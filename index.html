Why your salary number is almost never your real salary

When a company offers you $100,000 a year, that number is just the beginning of the calculation. Before you can understand what the offer is actually worth, you need to account for three major factors that most job seekers ignore entirely: cost of living differences, income taxes, and how the offer stacks up against what other people in your field are actually being paid.

Cost of living varies dramatically across cities. A $100,000 salary in San Francisco, where the cost of living index is among the highest in the country, gives you far less real purchasing power than the same salary in a city like Pittsburgh or Cincinnati. According to Numbeo's 2025 data, basic expenses in San Francisco run about 35% higher than the national average — meaning that $100k in San Francisco is worth roughly the same as $74,000 in a mid-cost city. That's a $26,000 difference on the same nominal salary.

Taxes compound the problem. A $100,000 salary in California comes with a 9.3% state income tax on top of federal taxes. The same salary in Texas comes with zero state income tax. That single difference can be worth $7,000–$9,000 per year in take-home pay — a bigger raise than most people get in an average year.

And then there's market positioning. Without knowing where your offer sits relative to what other people with your experience level, in your industry, at companies of similar size are actually being paid, you have no idea whether to negotiate or not — or how hard.

How cost of living adjustments work

Cost of living indices compare the relative cost of a basket of goods and services — housing, food, transportation, utilities, healthcare — across cities. We use Numbeo's 2025 Cost of Living Plus Rent Index, which is one of the most comprehensive and frequently updated databases available, covering thousands of data points contributed by users worldwide.

In this calculator, New York City serves as the baseline (index of 2.00). Cities with lower indices are cheaper — for example, Austin has an index of 1.33, meaning it's about 33% cheaper than New York on a combined cost-of-living-plus-rent basis. When we calculate your "real purchasing power," we're showing you what your salary would need to be in your home city to give you the equivalent lifestyle to what the offer would provide in the work city.

Understanding market percentiles

The market percentile tells you where your offer lands relative to what other people in your field are actually earning. A 50th percentile offer means half of people in comparable roles earn more, and half earn less. A 75th percentile offer means you'd be earning more than 75% of your peers.

Our benchmarks come from the Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics program (May 2024 release), the largest employer survey in the United States covering approximately 1.1 million business establishments. We supplement tech roles with Levels.fyi compensation data, which tracks total compensation including base, bonus, and equity at major technology companies.

The benchmarks are further adjusted by company size, because compensation varies significantly between a 10-person startup and a 50,000-person enterprise. FAANG and top-tier tech companies typically pay 40–50% above market median for equivalent roles, reflecting their ability to attract top talent.

Who this tool is for

This salary calculator is designed for anyone who has received a job offer and wants to understand its real value before responding. It's particularly useful for people who are comparing offers from companies in different cities, people who work remotely for a company headquartered in a high-cost city, people making their first major career move, and anyone who suspects their current salary may be below market but doesn't know by how much.

It's also useful for freelancers and consultants benchmarking an hourly rate against what full-time employees in their field are earning — you can compare what a $100/hour consulting rate implies about annual earnings versus typical full-time salaries for equivalent work.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the cost of living adjustment?

We use Numbeo's 2025 Cost of Living Plus Rent Index, one of the most widely cited real-time cost of living databases, updated continuously from crowdsourced price data across thousands of contributors per city. New York City serves as the 2.00 baseline. The adjustment gives you a directional read — for lifestyle-specific precision, cross-reference Numbeo's city comparison tool with your actual spending patterns.

Where does the market benchmark data come from?

Market medians are anchored to BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics from May 2024 — the most comprehensive government salary survey in the United States, covering approximately 1.1 million business establishments. For technology roles, we supplement with Levels.fyi compensation data. Company size multipliers reflect documented compensation premiums at different organization sizes: FAANG and top-tier tech companies typically pay 40–50% above market median for equivalent roles.

How is the take-home estimate calculated?

We apply 2024 federal income tax brackets, estimated state income tax for your work city, and FICA contributions (Social Security at 6.2% up to the 2024 wage cap of $168,600, plus Medicare at 1.45% on all earnings). We don't account for pre-tax deductions like 401(k) contributions or health insurance premiums — your actual take-home will typically be higher once those reduce your taxable income.

Does the calculator work for remote jobs?

Yes — select "Remote (US avg)" as the work location. Note that some companies now pay based on your home city's cost of living rather than a single flat rate, so check your offer letter carefully for any geographic pay adjustment clauses. If your company uses location-based pay, select your home city as both the work and home location for the most accurate calculation.

Does this work for international salary comparisons?

Yes — we cover 12 international cities across Canada, Europe, and Asia-Pacific. For international comparisons, we convert using CoL indices relative to NYC. Tax estimates for non-US cities are approximations only, as international tax systems vary enormously by country, filing status, and deductions available. The CoL purchasing power adjustment is accurate; the tax estimate should be verified with a local tax professional.

Should I always negotiate salary?

Yes. Research consistently shows that over 70% of hiring managers have some budget flexibility, and fewer than 40% of candidates ask. The worst realistic outcome is they say no and the offer stands. Use the counter-offer email we generate as a starting point — it's designed to be firm but professional, citing market data rather than personal need, which is the framing that works best in salary negotiations.

What's the difference between nominal salary and real purchasing power?

Your nominal salary is the number on the offer letter. Your real purchasing power is what that money can actually buy, adjusted for where you live. If you're offered $120,000 to work in San Francisco but you live in Austin, your real purchasing power is lower than $120,000 because San Francisco's cost of living is significantly higher than Austin's. The calculator shows you both numbers so you understand what the offer is actually worth in terms of your day-to-day life.

How do company size multipliers affect the market benchmark?

Compensation varies significantly by company size. Seed-stage startups typically pay 15–20% below market median (compensating with equity upside). Mid-size companies sit near the median. FAANG and top-tier tech companies pay 40–50% above median. Our benchmarks apply these multipliers to the BLS national medians so your comparison reflects what people at companies similar to yours are actually earning — not a blended average that includes everyone from tiny startups to trillion-dollar corporations.

How often is the data updated?

Cost of living data is sourced from Numbeo's 2025 annual dataset. Salary benchmarks use BLS OES May 2024 figures, which are the most recent available at time of publication. We review and update all data annually. For the most current figures, cross-reference with BLS directly at bls.gov/oes and Numbeo at numbeo.com.

Can I use this to evaluate my current salary, not just a new offer?

Absolutely. Enter your current salary and city exactly as you would for a new offer. The market percentile will tell you where you currently stand. If you're below the 40th percentile, you have a strong case for asking for a raise — use the counter-offer email as a template for that conversation too, adjusted to address your current employer rather than a new one.
