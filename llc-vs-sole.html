Pros & cons at a glance

✅ Sole Proprietor

  • Zero setup cost or paperwork
  • Simplest tax filing (Schedule C)
  • No annual state fees
  • Easy to dissolve

⚠️ Watch out

  • Personal liability for business debts
  • Full self-employment tax on all profit
  • Harder to raise investment

✅ LLC

  • Personal liability protection
  • More credible to clients & banks
  • S-Corp election can reduce SE tax
  • Flexible profit distribution

⚠️ Watch out

  • State filing fees ($50–$800/yr)
  • More complex bookkeeping
  • S-Corp requires payroll setup

Common questions

When does an LLC actually save money on taxes?

A default LLC (disregarded entity) saves nothing on taxes versus a sole proprietor — they're taxed identically. The savings come when you elect S-Corporation status, which lets you split income between a salary and distributions. You only pay self-employment tax (15.3%) on the salary, not the distributions. This typically becomes worthwhile around $60–80k+ in net profit.

What's a "reasonable salary" for S-Corp purposes?

The IRS requires S-Corp owners to pay themselves a "reasonable salary" for the work they do — similar to what they'd pay someone else in that role. There's no fixed formula, but it should be defensible if audited. Most accountants suggest 40–60% of net profit as a starting point. Too low a salary is a red flag for IRS audits.

Do I need a lawyer to form an LLC?

No. You can file directly with your state's Secretary of State website for $50–$500 depending on the state. Wyoming and Delaware are popular for favorable laws and low fees. However, an accountant or CPA is worth consulting before making the S-Corp election — the payroll setup has ongoing costs that need to exceed the tax savings.

Is this comparison accurate for my state?

This calculator uses federal tax rates plus an approximate state income tax rate. It doesn't account for state-specific LLC fees (California charges $800/year minimum, for example), franchise taxes, or local taxes. Treat it as a directional estimate and verify with a local accountant for your specific situation.
